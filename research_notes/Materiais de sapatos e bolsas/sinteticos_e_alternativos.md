# Synthetic and alternative "leather" materials in shoes and bags

## Q1. How PU, PVC, microfibre, plant-based "vegan leathers" and cork perform: lifespan, breathability, care, ageing

### Takeaway
Almost every "vegan leather" sold today is a coated textile: a polyester (sometimes cellulose) fabric with polyurethane layers, and sometimes plant filler mixed in. Independent lab testing (FILK Freiberg, 2021) found that none of them matches leather across the board. They breathe roughly 2 to 10 times less than leather, and several (Desserto, Appleskin, Vegea) crack under flexing much earlier than leather. Standard PU usually starts peeling within about 2 to 5 years through hydrolysis. PVC stiffens and cracks as its plasticizer migrates out.

### Cited Findings
**Peer-reviewed head-to-head test: Meyer et al., FILK Freiberg, *Coatings* 2021, 11, 226**
- Materials tested: full-grain shoe-upper leather, a PUR-coated textile (standard synthetic), Desserto, Kombucha, Piñatex, Noani, Appleskin, Vegea, SnapPap, Teak Leaf and Muskin. Measured: ISO tensile strength, tear, water vapour permeability (WVP, ISO 14268), water vapour absorption (WVA) and flex resistance (ISO 32100) — [Meyer et al. 2021, Coatings](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/11/2/226)
- Construction as found under the microscope and by FTIR — [same](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/11/2/226):
  - **Desserto**: polyester textile carrier with a compact PUR layer and a partially foamed PUR layer filled with organic particles.
  - **Appleskin**: thin compact PUR layers, a foamed PUR layer with organic filler, and a PUR-impregnated textile carrier.
  - **Vegea**: compact and foamed PUR layers on a cellulose textile carrier.
  - **Piñatex**: a nonwoven of cellulose fibres with a thin polymeric coating "similar to polyurethane acrylate". The intro mentions a PLA coating. Separately, the maker states the coating is a water-based PU resin — [FashionUnited summary](https://fashionunited.com/news/fashion/why-leather-alternatives-don-t-necessarily-meet-animal-friendly-standards/2022111850790).
  - **Noani**: polyester microfibre on top, leather fibreboard in the core, PUR-coated textile on the back.
  - **Muskin** (fungus): a single uncoated layer of natural polysaccharide fibre.
  - The authors say Desserto, Vegea and Appleskin "reflect the typical composition of PUR coated artificial leather".
- Test results (Table, Meyer et al. 2021):

| Material | Tensile strength (N/mm²) | Tear (N/mm) | WVP (mg/cm²·h) | Flex cycles to grade 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leather | 39.5 | 82.9 | 4.6 | >200,000 |
| PUR-coated textile | 10.2 | 17 | 1.1 | 200,000 |
| Desserto | 20.8 | 37.2 | 0.5 | 30,000 |
| Appleskin | 14 | 18.4 | 0.4 | 50,000 |
| Vegea | 9.4 | 16.6 | 0.6 | 50,000 |
| Piñatex | 4.5 | 31 | 2.5 | 150,000 |
| Teak Leaf | 12.2 | 30.7 | 0.1 | 100 |
| Kombucha | 9.7 | 5.1 | 0.1 | 10,000 |
| Muskin | 0.2 | 0.5 | 10.4 | 10,000 |

  Source: [Meyer et al. 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/11/2/226)
- Only leather, Piñatex and the PUR-coated textile passed the shoe-upper flex specification of more than 80,000 cycles (ISO 20942). The flex resistance of Teak Leaf, SnapPap and Muskin was "insufficient" — [same](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/11/2/226)
- Conclusion: "None of the alternative materials achieved the properties of leather according to the applied reference values." The authors attribute leather's all-round performance to its grown multilayer structure with a density gradient — [same](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/11/2/226)
- The same study found restricted substances in Desserto: butanone oxime, toluene, free isocyanate, folpet and traces of DIBP. This is reported second-hand — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/fashion/why-leather-alternatives-don-t-necessarily-meet-animal-friendly-standards/2022111850790)
- Desserto's plant content is disputed. Leather UK estimates it is about 65% polyurethane. Other reporting says the plant share ranges from 30% to 90% depending on the application — [search-summarised reporting via FashionUnited / the-ethos](https://the-ethos.co/your-vegan-leather-bag-is-mostly-petroleum/)

**PU ageing: hydrolysis and delamination**
- Hydrolysis is water breaking down the PU polymer. It shows up as the PU film delaminating from its textile backing — [Engineerfix](https://engineerfix.com/how-to-repair-pu-leather-fix-peeling-cracks-and-tears/); [Ajyibo (supplier)](https://www.ajyibo.com/knowledge/is-pu-fabric-resistant-to-cracking-and-peeling-over-time)
- Typical lifespan figures come from retailers and suppliers only: PU usually lasts 3 to 5 years before peeling or cracking, and less in humid climates — [Castlery](https://www.castlery.com/us/blog/what-is-pu-leather). Faux leather (PU/PVC) lasts 2 to 5 years — [Decrum](https://decrum.ca/blogs/news/faux-leather-vs-real-leather). Polycarbonate PU can last 7 to 10 years, while standard PU may break down in 2 to 3 years in damp conditions — [Ajyibo (vendor)](https://www.ajyibo.com/knowledge/is-pu-fabric-resistant-to-cracking-and-peeling-over-time)
- A faux-leather manufacturer warns that hydrolysis "jungle test" weeks do not translate into years of field life. Treat "X weeks = X years" marketing with caution — [Mitchell Faux Leathers](https://mitchellfauxleathers.com/home/faux-leather-viewpoint/viewpoint/2017/03/27/hydrolysis-testing-methods); for the opposing claim see [Materialised/Ultrafabrics](https://materialised.com.au/ultrafabrics-faux-leather-hydrolysis-resistance-and-specification/)
- On cheap goods, poor bonding between the PU skin and the substrate causes early peeling — [Ajyibo](https://www.ajyibo.com/knowledge/is-pu-fabric-resistant-to-cracking-and-peeling-over-time)

**PVC ageing**
- Pure PVC is rigid and brittle, so plasticizers are added to make it flexible. They migrate out over time through volatilisation, exudation or extraction, which is a main cause of early coating failure — [J. Leather Sci. Eng. 2022 review](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s42825-022-00081-8)
- Conventional plasticizers "suffer from fast leaching", which shortens the life of the artificial material. Phthalate plasticizers are being restricted because of health concerns — [same](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s42825-022-00081-8)

**Microfibre synthetic leather**
- Conventional film-on-fabric synthetics do not breathe like leather. Microfibre nonwovens impregnated with PU, especially with open-cell or porous layers, can approach leather in a manufacturer's own tests. One patent reports 607 g/m²·24h for its material against 557 for its leather reference — [USPTO patent 11155960](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11155960); [USPTO 9644312](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9644312)

**Mycelium**
- Bolt Threads paused production of Mylo (mycelium leather) in mid-2023 after failing to raise scale-up funding. Mylo had partnered with Stella McCartney and Adidas. MycoWorks and Ecovative continue — [Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/news/sustainability/bolt-threads-mylo-alternative-leather-mushroom-pause-operations/); [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/bolt-threads-reportedly-pulls-plug-on-stella-mccartney-backed-mylo/2023070454659)

**Cork**
- Cork fabric is cork shaved to about 0.5 to 1 mm and bonded to a backing, often a polyester, cotton and PU blend. The backing determines both strength and how sustainable the material is — [Sustainable Jungle](https://www.sustainablejungle.com/sustainable-fashion/what-is-cork-fabric); [Corkor (vendor)](https://www.corkor.com/blogs/corkor/how-durable-is-cork-fabric)
- Cork is water-repellent thanks to suberin, but it is not waterproof. It is generally less durable than cowhide under heavy long-term use. A bag maker documented 3 years of steady use — [Sustainable Jungle](https://www.sustainablejungle.com/sustainable-fashion/what-is-cork-fabric); [Tom Bihn](https://www.tombihn.com/blogs/main/photos-little-swift-in-cork-after-3-years-of-steady-use)

### Inferences
- For scoring purposes, treat Desserto, Appleskin, Vegea and most "apple / grape / cactus / corn leather" as PU-coated textiles. Expect the same hydrolysis risk as PU and lower breathability than leather. The "bio" share is a filler or partial polyol and does not change the failure mode.
- Breathability ranking from FILK WVP: leather (4.6) > Piñatex (2.5) > standard PUR synthetic (1.1) > Vegea, Desserto, Appleskin (0.4 to 0.6). For shoe linings and uppers, sweat comfort is worst with fruit and cactus PU materials.
- In bags, flex matters less than in shoe uppers, so PU bags typically fail at edges, handles and corners through peeling. Shoes fail at the toe flex crease.
- PVC: expect stiffening, brittleness and cracking, worse with cold and heat. It is cheaper and less supple than PU.
- Care, generalised: wipe with a damp cloth, avoid heat, solvents and long damp storage. Leather conditioners do not help PU or PVC. Hydrolysis cannot be reversed. This is general practice and was not sourced to a lab.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed field-lifespan data (years) for PU or PVC. All year figures are from vendors or retailers.
- No independent test data for microfibre synthetic versus leather beyond patents. Cork has no lab data; vendor claims only.
- MycoWorks (Reishi) and other current mycelium products were not tested here.

## Q2. Misleading or ambiguous store terms (PT, ES, FR, EN)

### Takeaway
All of these terms describe non-animal material, almost always PU or PVC on a textile: "faux leather", "leatherette", "vegan leather", "PU leather", "eco-leather", "piel sintética", "polipiel", "simili cuir", "cuir vegan", "couro ecológico", "couro sintético", "pele sintética" and "efeito pele". "Eco/ecológico" does not mean plant-based. Several of these terms are illegal in their own markets (see Q3), but they remain widespread online.

### Cited Findings
- The FILK paper notes that the market uses many terms for PVC- or PU-coated polyester textiles: "artificial leather, synthetic leather, leatherette, imitation leather…". It calls these completely fossil-based materials with an embossed grain — [Meyer et al. 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/11/2/226)
- France's DGCCRF recorded "cuir vegan" and "simili cuir" among the fanciful designations on garments in its 2018 inspections. The French ministry says "cuir vegan" and "cuir végétal" are not admissible — [DGCCRF fiche "le cuir"](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques/le-cuir); [Assemblée nationale QE](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QE37838.pdf)
- In Brazil, "couro sintético" and "couro ecológico" are banned by law but still widely used online — [Boca](https://boca.com.br/justica/uso-de-termos-como-couro-sintetico-e-proibido-no-brasil)
- In Spain, RD 769/1984 forbids "piel/cuero" even with "artificial / sintético / simulado". In practice this pushed the trade towards the coined word "polipiel" (poly + piel) — [BOE RD 769/1984](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/1984/02/08/769/con). The link between the decree and "polipiel" is my inference.

### Inferences
- Map these terms to PU/PVC synthetic by default (high confidence): faux leather, leatherette, PU leather, vegan leather, eco-leather, synthetic leather, simili cuir, similicuir, cuir vegan, cuir synthétique, piel sintética, polipiel, piel vegana, ecopiel, couro sintético, couro ecológico, couro vegano, pele sintética, napa sintética, "material sintético".
- "Efeito pele", "efecto piel", "aspect cuir", "façon cuir", "leather-look" and "leather-effect" describe appearance only. The material is synthetic unless stated otherwise.
- "Napa / nappa" alone is ambiguous. In real leather it means a soft grain, but Brazilian and Iberian retail also uses "napa" for PU.
- "Bonded leather", "couro reconstituído" or "cuero regenerado" means ground leather fibres with a binder, often PU-faced. Score it as low durability.
- "Microfibra" or "microfibre leather" usually means a PU-impregnated microfibre nonwoven. It is better than basic PU-on-knit, but still synthetic.
- Apple, cactus, grape and corn "leather" should be treated as PU or bio-PU composites (Q1).
- Ecuador/Ecopel and similar brand names should be checked case by case.

### Gaps
- No consumer-agency study quantifying how often these terms appear or mislead shoppers.

## Q3. Regulation on calling non-leather "leather"

### Takeaway
The EU harmonises only footwear labelling (Directive 94/11/EC), which reserves the leather pictogram and term for tanned hide. Coatings over 0.15 mm make it "coated leather". Several member states go further with national bans: Italy (2020), Portugal (2022), France (2010) and Spain (1984). Brazil has banned "couro" for non-animal material since 1965, with a stricter law in 2005. In the UK the ASA treats calling non-100% leather "leather" as misleading.

### Cited Findings
- **EU, Directive 94/11/EC (footwear)**: "leather" is hide or skin that keeps its original fibrous structure and is tanned to be rot-proof. A surface coating must be 0.15 mm or less. Thicker coatings (up to one third of total thickness) must be labelled "coated leather". The labelling covers the upper, the lining/sock and the outsole, and retailers are responsible for it — [EUR-Lex 94/11/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1994/11/oj/eng); [legislation.gov.uk copy](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/1994/11/data.html)
- **Portugal, Decreto-Lei 3/2022**: defines "couro/pele" and restricts its commercial use. Terms like "vegan leather" or "synthetic leather" are no longer allowed, and fines are reported — [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-us/e-ssentials-newsletter/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/e-ssentials-4-2022/portugal-enacted-the-decree-on-the-use-of-leather-and-related-terminologies); [SGS SafeGuard 057/22](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2022/05/safeguards-05722-portugals-leather-decree-comes-into-force); [Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/vegan-leather-term-banned-in-portugal/)
- **Italy, Decree 68/2020**: bans leather terms in any language, including as prefixes or suffixes, for non-animal materials. In force 24 Oct 2020, with a sell-off until 23 Oct 2022. Footwear is excluded because it falls under 94/11/EC — [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-us/e-ssentials-newsletter/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/e-ssentials-12-2020/italy-approved-the-new-regulation-on-leather-material-and-product-labelling)
- **France, Décret 2010-29**: bans "façon cuir", "simili-cuir", "imitation cuir" and "faux cuir" for non-leather. Footwear and PPE are excluded. The rule does not apply to goods lawfully made in another EU state — [DGCCRF](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques/le-cuir); [Legifrance](https://legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000021645894/)
- **Spain, RD 769/1984**: "piel", "cuero" and "curtido" are reserved for animal skin and cannot be used with "imitado, reconstituido, simulado, artificial, sintético" — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/1984/02/08/769/con). Current in-force status was not verified.
- **Brazil, Lei 4.888/1965** bans selling non-animal products "sob o nome de couro" — [Planalto](https://planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/1950-1969/L4888.htm). **Lei 11.211/2005** bans "couro" and its derivatives, even in a foreign language, for non-animal materials in footwear and goods — [Câmara](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei/2005/lei-11211-19-dezembro-2005-539653-publicacaooriginal-39026-pl.html); [Global Legal Post](https://www.globallegalpost.com/news/beyond-leather-brazils-material-labelling-rules-for-luxury-brands-908373839)
- **UK ASA**: marketers must not call a product "leather" unless it is 100% leather. "Premium Bonded Leather" was ruled misleading (Love Sofas). The ASA points to the BS leather glossary — [ASA "making sure your ad isn't given a hiding"](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/making-sure-your-ad-isnt-given-a-hiding.html); [ASA Love Sofas ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/love-sofas-ltd-a15-290800.html)
- **ISO 15115:2019** is the international leather vocabulary. It was confirmed in 2025 and a revision is in progress — [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/67424.html)

### Inferences
- The extension can flag "couro ecológico", "couro sintético", "cuir vegan" and "pele sintética" on BR, PT and FR stores as both synthetic and non-compliant wording. That is a useful trust signal about the seller.
- For EU footwear, the legally binding signal is the 94/11 pictogram or label. "Coated leather" means a thick finish that breathes less.

### Gaps
- No ASA ruling found that specifically addresses "vegan leather" or "faux leather".
- The full Portuguese decree text was not read (fetch blocked), so the exact list of banned terms and whether "efeito pele" is covered remain unconfirmed.
- Spain's RD 769/1984 current status and the role of UNE norms are unverified.
- No current EU-level proposal to protect the term "leather" was found.
