# Textile uppers/bags and sole materials (shoes)

## Uppers and bags: canvas/cotton, polyester knit/mesh, nylon

### Takeaway
Most sources say high-denier nylon resists abrasion and tearing best, polyester handles UV and wet conditions better, and cotton canvas comes last unless it is waxed or coated. In shoe uppers, tightly woven mesh usually resists scuffs better than knit, and knit snags more easily. Almost no independent lab data exists here; most of what is published is commercial content.

### Cited Findings
- High-denier nylon (600D+) is the usual recommendation for scuff and puncture resistance. Denier measures fibre thickness, so it only compares fabrics of the same material. For canvas, weight is the comparable measure, typically 12–16 oz/yd². — [Canvas ETC: nylon vs polyester](https://www.canvasetc.com/nylon-vs-polyester-what-are-the-differences/)
- Martindale claims: nylon often above 50,000 cycles, polyester 30,000–60,000. These are unverified manufacturer-blog figures. — [szoneier](https://szoneier.com/?p=42750)
- Polyester resists UV better, absorbs less water and keeps its shape when wet. Sources disagree on whether nylon handles UV better or worse; some say nylon includes UV stabilisers. — [Canvas ETC](https://www.canvasetc.com/nylon-vs-polyester-what-are-the-differences/); compare [CabinZero](https://www.cabinzero.com/blogs/travel-blog/what-are-backpacks-made-of)
- Cotton canvas is more vulnerable to moisture, mould and UV. Its tight weave gives decent abrasion resistance, and waxing or coating improves it. — [CabinZero](https://www.cabinzero.com/blogs/travel-blog/what-are-backpacks-made-of)
- Knit durability depends on how dense the knit is. Dense knit, mesh with overlays, and leather are the more durable options. Open engineered knit is the most breathable. — [TheFitVille: upper materials](https://thefitville.com/pages/faq-new-walking-shoe-upper-materials-explained)
- RunRepeat has a guide on choosing upper materials, covering knit, mesh and others. — [RunRepeat upper guide](https://runrepeat.com/guides/running-shoe-upper-how-to-choose-the-best-one-for-your-needs)
- Mesh is said to resist curb scuffs better, and knit is said to snag more, but these come from low-quality Alibaba "product insight" pages. — [Alibaba insight](https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/mesh-vs-knit-running-shoes-does-the-upper-really-make-a-difference-for-your-run.html)

### Inferences
- Scoring suggestion for abrasion: nylon (high denier) ≥ polyester > coated or waxed canvas > plain cotton canvas. For sun and rain exposure, polyester gets a bonus and cotton a penalty.
- How each material ages: cotton canvas fades, stains and can grow mould. Knit pills, snags and loosens, and toe holes are a typical failure. Mesh tears at flex points. Synthetics look the same for longer but cannot be repaired.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, standardised Martindale numbers comparing these materials in footwear. SATRA, CTCP and INESCOP publish only test methods, not comparisons a consumer can use.
- I found no source with numeric breathability data (water-vapour permeability) for knit versus mesh versus canvas.

## Soles: rubber, EVA, TPU, PU, crepe, leather, cork

### Takeaway
Rubber outsoles last longest and grip best. Harder rubber is more durable but grips less. EVA midsoles lose a measurable part of their cushioning at about 480–640 km of running. PU soles break down chemically through hydrolysis in roughly 5–10 years even when unworn, and faster in heat and humidity. Crepe and leather wear quickly on hard ground, and leather slips in the wet. Cork lasts several years if it is resealed regularly.

### Cited Findings
- **Rubber outsole:** RunRepeat tests wear with a Dremel and measures dent depth. The average dent is about 1.1 mm. Harder rubber (measured in HC units) is more durable, and grippy outsoles often use softer rubber that wears faster. Across more than 200 shoes, it found no strong link between grip and durability; rubber quality and how much of the sole the rubber covers matter more. — [RunRepeat outsole insights](https://runrepeat.com/guides/insights-into-road-and-trail-running-shoe-outsoles); [RunRepeat durable sneakers](https://www.runrepeat.com/guides/best-durable-sneakers)
- Footwear rubber abrasion loss (DIN 53516) is about 140–300 mm³ in one patent. Lower means more wear-resistant. Filled SBR compounds can reach below 150 mm³. — [USPTO patent 10364336](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10364336); [Turkchem](https://turkchem.net/en/makale/ayakkabi-polimerleri)
- **TPU:** An abrasion-resistant TPU grade loses less than 45 mm³ (DIN 53516), which the supplier says is comparable to rubber. This is a supplier claim. — [Trinseo APILON 52](https://www.trinseo.com/solutions/thermoplastic-polyurethanes/apilon-52-ac-thermoabrasion-resistant-tpu)
- **EVA:** EVA wears faster than rubber when used as a ground-contact surface. — [Blister glossary](https://blisterreview.com/gear-101/trail-running-glossary-of-terms-trail-running-shoe-materials-tech)
- EVA cushioning loss, in-vivo study: rearfoot strikers lost about 16–33% of heel cushioning after 480 km. Other studies place the loss between 480 and 640 km. — [PMC5534152](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5534152)
- EVA, heel-shoe interaction study: peak plantar pressure rose about 100% after 500 km. The foam showed structural damage (wrinkling and holes) after 750 km. — [PubMed 15275845](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15275845/)
- Compression set test: the foam is compressed for 22 hours, then allowed to recover for 24 hours. No foam returns to 0% set. — [RunRepeat foams guide](https://www.runrepeat.com/uk/guides/running-shoe-foams-guide)
- After 450 km, denser EVA held up better than PEBA. Energy return: EVA about 66%, TPU about 76%, PEBA about 87%. — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/midsole-foams/); [Outside](https://www.outsideonline.com/health/training-performance/how-long-do-supershoes-last-research/?scope=anon)
- **PU hydrolysis:** water vapour breaks the polymer chains, and heat and humidity speed this up. Basic PU lasts about 1.5 years in tropical conditions and about 3 years in normal conditions, according to a vendor claim. Polyether PU resists hydrolysis better than polyester PU. Hydrolysed PU cannot be repaired. — [Safety Jogger](https://safetyjogger.com/en/academy/hydrolysis-crumbling-shoe-soles-explained)
- Uvex found disintegrated soles 7–14 years after manufacture. Storage can do more damage than wearing. — [Uvex blog](https://www.uvex-safety.com/blog/why-do-shoe-soles-disintegrate/)
- PU soles typically last 5–10 years before hydrolysis. Wearing them regularly and storing them cool and dry extends this. — [Kuraray](https://www.elastomer.kuraray.com/blog/polyurethane-soles/)
- PU abrasion life is said to be 1.5–3 times that of rubber or EVA. This is an industry article and conflicts with the general view that rubber is the most durable. — [Turkchem](https://turkchem.net/en/makale/ayakkabi-polimerleri)
- **Crepe:** it is soft and comfortable but breaks down on concrete. Its lifespan depends on how thick it is. Sources disagree on wet grip: one says it is slippery, another says it grips better than leather but worse than rubber. — [Stridewise: crepe soles](https://stridewise.com/crepe-soles/); [Walk London](https://www.walklondonshoes.com/en-ee/blogs/news/what-is-a-crepe-sole)
- **Leather sole:** on tarmac it wears faster than rubber and slips more. A thin rubber layer added on top improves both wear and grip. — [Stridewise: leather soles](https://stridewise.com/pros-cons-leather-soles); [The Shoe Snob](https://theshoesnobblog.com/leather-soles-vs-rubber-soles/)
- **Cork footbed:** it dries out and crumbles from the edges. Reseal it when it loses its shine, about twice a year. Keep it away from direct heat. If it breaks down too far, the footbed and the EVA sole have to be replaced together. — [Birkenstock AU FAQ](https://www.birkenstock.com.au/pages/faq-why-is-the-cork-dry-and-crumbling); [Birkenstock NZ repair](https://www.birkenstock.co.nz/blogs/journal/can-birkenstocks-be-repaired-a-guide-to-resoling-refurbishing-longevity)

### Inferences
- Suggested durability ranking for the outsole (the part touching the ground): rubber ≈ TPU > PU > crepe ≈ leather > exposed EVA.
- A PU sole carries a time-based risk on top of wear: shoes stored for a long time (old stock, "deadstock") can crumble without ever being worn. A useful warning for the extension would be "PU sole: avoid storing in humid places; lasts years, not decades."
- Practical rule for EVA: running shoes lose cushioning at roughly 500–800 km. For walking or everyday wear, I found no km figure.

### Gaps
- I found no source with km or month lifespans for casual sneakers or walking shoes; all the km data comes from running.
- I found no DIN abrasion value in mm³ for EVA, and no Birkenstock lifespan in years.
- I did not consult SATRA, CTCP or INESCOP reports directly; their consumer-facing data is limited or paywalled.
